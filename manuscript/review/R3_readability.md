# Reviewer 3 Report — Readability and Journal Fit

**Manuscript:** "Who measures what enters the market? Self-organised variety trials and the third-party-assayed grain-quality gap in China's rice variety approvals, 2017–2022"
**Target journal:** Journal of Integrative Agriculture (JIA)
**Reviewer focus:** structure/flow, figure/table self-sufficiency, abstract-body consistency, journal-format compliance, accessibility to a non-specialist reader

---

## Overall assessment

The manuscript is unusually disciplined about scope and unusually explicit about what it does and does not claim — this is a real strength for a regulatory-economics paper making a sensitive comparison. However, at **14,691 words** of body text it runs 47–84% over the journal's own stated norm of 8,000–10,000 words (`plan/04_format_spec.md` §1), and a first-time reader will need real effort to get through the accumulated hedges, non-claims, and robustness detail before reaching the substantive contribution. The paper is fundamentally soundly organised — the section sequence is logical and each section does what its heading promises — but it is written at the length and register of an internal audit trail rather than a submission-ready journal article. Compression is achievable without cutting any declared result; it mainly requires moving prose-level detail into tables and consolidating repeated statements of the same caveat.

## Major strengths

1. **Logical macro-structure.** Introduction → Institutional background → Data → Empirical strategy → Results → Robustness → Mechanism → Discussion → Conclusion is a natural progression, and each section transitions cleanly into the next (e.g., §4.7's "What this paper does not claim" sets up exactly the boundary conditions that §5–7 then respect, and §8.2's "closing window" discussion follows directly from §5.5/§5.6's convergence findings).
2. **Terms are defined at first use.** Self-organised trial, green channel (绿色通道), consortium trial (联合体区域试验), NY/T 593, and composition effect are all defined in the Introduction or §2 before being used analytically (see line-by-line check below). A non-specialist reader unfamiliar with Chinese seed regulation can follow the institutional setup.
3. **The abstract is a faithful, appropriately hedged compression of the body**, including the "(suggestive only)" qualifier on the yield result — this is the item most likely to be softened away in a careless abstract, and it survives intact (see §3 below).
4. **Figures are generally self-sufficient.** Most panels carry enough legend/axis/annotation text to be read without the prose (detailed in §2 below).

## Major concerns

1. **Manuscript length (14,691 words) is 47–84% over the journal's 8,000–10,000-word target** (`plan/04_format_spec.md` §1). This is the single largest readability problem in the draft; see the compression recommendations below.
2. **Section 6 (Robustness, placebos and bounds) is the longest section in the paper (≈3,297 words, ~22% of the body)** and reads as a checklist walkthrough (R1 through R15, each with its own paragraph, several repeating the same headline coefficients already given in §5). A reader who has already absorbed Table 3 does not need the −1.844/+1.108/−0.122 triad re-quoted in §6.3, §6.4, §6.5, §6.6, and again in §6.15 — five repetitions of the same three numbers.
3. **Section 4 (Empirical strategy, 2,482 words) is disproportionately long relative to its function**, largely because of §4.7 ("What this paper does not claim," ~1,050 words on its own). Ten numbered non-claims, several running to 4–6 sentences with their own supporting statistics, is unusual for what is conventionally a short list. Some of this content duplicates material that appears again, near-verbatim, in the Discussion's Limitations (§8.4) and the Conclusion.
4. **Repetition of the same numeric evidence across sections** is the main driver of excess length, not the number of distinct findings. The pattern "state the number in §5, restate it in §6 as a robustness cross-check, restate it a third time in the summary paragraph, restate it a fourth time in the Discussion/Conclusion" recurs for the headline head-rice/chalkiness/quality-grade/yield-gain coefficients.
5. **Figure 4 (breakpoint scan) includes a "Growth duration (days)" series that is never discussed in the corresponding text (§5.6).** The text discusses trial yield (2017), head-rice (2015), chalkiness (2009), and quality grade (2015), but the figure's most visually dominant line (growth duration, peaking near 2018, drawn in a color that dominates the upper part of the plot) has no textual referent anywhere in §5.6 or its caption in `figure_table_list.md`. A reader looking at the figure alone will not know why growth duration is included or what its break year means for the argument.
6. **The figure/table compression arithmetic does not close**, as `figure_table_list.md` itself flags: the format spec calls for 6 main-text figures, but 7 are currently planned (Fig. 1–7), and no single document identifies the seventh cut. This is a concrete, fixable gap the author team must resolve before submission — it is not just a readability nicety, since JIA's figure count is a formatting requirement, not a suggestion.

## Technical failings (readability/format-specific)

- **Word count**: 14,691 words vs. the 8,000–10,000 target in `04_format_spec.md` §1 — a hard compliance gap against the format spec's own guidance, even though JIA's official guide does not publish a hard ceiling.
- **Highlights**: all five highlights are at or under the 85-character limit per `sections/front_matter.md`'s self-check (79/71/78/77/83 characters) — compliant. (Note: `figure_table_list.md`'s and `04_format_spec.md`'s highlight-4 wording differ slightly from the version actually used in `manuscript_v1.md`/`front_matter.md` — "diverge and are estimated separately" vs. "effects diverge and must be estimated separately" — a minor internal inconsistency across planning documents, not a compliance failure since the version actually in the manuscript is what will be submitted.)
- **Keywords**: 6 keywords, within the 3–6 range, none containing "and/of" as an internal connector — compliant per the front-matter self-check.
- **Abstract length**: `front_matter.md` reports 249 words, within the ≤250-word structured-abstract limit — compliant, though right at the boundary with no margin for later edits.
- **Declarations**: Acknowledgements and CRediT author contributions are explicit placeholders; Conflict of interest and Ethical approval are complete and appropriately worded ("declare no conflict of interest"; "Not applicable"). Data availability statement is present and its restrictive wording (parsing scripts/dictionary/IDs only, not the full parsed table) is deliberate and explained. These are all present in the required JIA order (Acknowledgements → Conflict of interest → Data availability → CRediT → Ethical approval → References), matching `04_format_spec.md` §1's structure, though the official minimal template only requires Acknowledgements/Conflict of interest/Ethical approval/References — the extra sections are additions the format spec explicitly permits.
- **Figures**: main-text figure count (7) exceeds the format spec's stated target (6), an unresolved compression gap flagged in `figure_table_list.md` §"Submission-target compression" itself.

## Figure-by-figure self-sufficiency assessment (Fig. 1–7, published numbering)

**Fig. 1 (channel-stacked area chart, 2005–2022).** Self-sufficient. Title, legend (three channels, with Chinese/English channel names implicit from the manuscript but the figure itself uses only English category labels — "Unified," "Green channel (self-organised)," "Consortium (joint trial)" — good for a non-Chinese-reading audience), and five vertical event markers with dated labels. A reader unfamiliar with the paper's text can infer the shape of the reform's rollout without reading §2, though the specific meaning of "green channel" vs. "consortium" as regulatory categories still requires the prose for full understanding — acceptable for a descriptive figure.

**Fig. 2 (main forest plot, both arms, 17 outcomes).** Mostly self-sufficient. Title states the fixed-effect structure and clustering choice; color legend distinguishes third-party-assayed vs. applicant-self-reported outcomes, which is the paper's central identification device — this is a real strength, since the color coding alone conveys the sign-separation argument visually. Two outcomes are marked "not estimable" in Arm 2 with an annotation, which is good practice. Minor gap: the outcome labels ("Yield gain vs check (pp, trial)" etc.) assume the reader already knows what "pp" means and what "check" refers to; this is defined in the text but not on the figure itself — a caveat, not a failure, for a dense multi-outcome forest plot.

**Fig. 3 (event-study, four-panel line plot).** Self-sufficient for its four displayed traits (head-rice, chalkiness, stated quality grade, yield gain over check), each with its own y-axis label, and two vertical dashed lines marking the 2021 standards revision and the 2022-08 rectification notice. The panels lack error-bar-crossing-zero annotations or an explicit "not significant" marker for years where the CI crosses zero, so a reader would need to inspect each point manually to judge significance — a minor legibility gap, not a critical failure since CIs are shown.

**Fig. 4 (sup-Wald breakpoint scan).** **Not fully self-sufficient.** The plot shows six lines (trial yield, head-rice, chalkiness, quality grade, yield gain, and growth duration) with break-year labels attached to only some of them (2009, 2015, 2015, 2017/2018, 2018), and the growth-duration series — visually the second-most prominent curve — is never mentioned in the corresponding text (§5.6) or given a labeled peak. A reader relying on the figure alone cannot tell why growth duration is plotted or what conclusion, if any, follows from its break point. This should either be removed from the figure or given a one-clause explanation in the caption and the text.

**Fig. 5 (published Fig. 5 = randomisation-inference null distributions, sourced from `fig6_randomization.png` per the renumbering table).** Self-sufficient. Four panels each state the observed coefficient and RI p-value directly on the panel, removing any need to consult the text for the headline numbers. This is the best-designed figure in the set for standalone reading.

**Fig. 6 (published Fig. 6 = missingness-balance dumbbell plot, sourced from `fig7_missingness_balance.png`).** Self-sufficient. Title states the imbalance threshold rule directly; axis and legend are clear; the outcome ordering (best-to-worst balanced, top to bottom) makes the pattern easy to read without prose. This is a strong candidate, as `figure_table_list.md` itself suggests, for downgrading to a table-only presentation, since Table 2 already reports the same missing rates numerically — but as a standalone figure it succeeds on self-sufficiency grounds.

**Fig. 7 (published Fig. 7 = Winall mechanism, three panels, sourced from `fig5_winall_mechanism.png`).** Mostly self-sufficient, and the best-captioned figure in the set — its title explicitly states the counter-case framing ("Winall enters this paper as a counter-case that rules out an alternative explanation, not as a source of the main result"), each of the three sub-panels has an explanatory second-line caption, and panel (c) explicitly marks imputed/estimated points with hollow markers plus a legend entry. One legibility issue: panel (c)'s dual y-axes (order-grain revenue share in orange, R&D intensity in blue) plus two vertical event lines plus two red text annotations create a busy panel that a first read may take some time to parse, though every element is labeled.

## Compression recommendations (to reach 8,000–10,000 words)

Given the section-level word counts (Introduction 1,245; Institutional background 718; Data 1,526; Empirical strategy 2,482; Results 1,910; Robustness 3,297; Mechanism 928; Discussion 1,325; Conclusion 750 — total body 14,691 excluding front matter/references), the following cuts target roughly 5,000–6,500 words of reduction without deleting any declared result, failure, or non-claim:

1. **Section 6 (Robustness) — largest single opportunity, target cutting ~1,500–2,000 words.** Convert R1–R14's individual narrative paragraphs into a leaner format: state the check, state the result, and refer to Table 4 for the numbers already required to be tabulated there, rather than re-quoting full coefficient/CI/p triples in prose for checks (R1–R6, R8, R12–R14) that do not qualify or reverse the headline result. Keep full prose treatment only for checks that *do* qualify or complicate the main finding — R7 (Manski bounds, sign instability), R9 (within-applicant, underpowered), R11 (provincial, mixed result), and R15 (chained-check ladder, genuinely novel method requiring explanation) — since these are the ones a reader actually needs explained rather than tabulated. This alone should not remove any of the fifteen checks or any qualifying/failing result; it only moves already-tabulated numbers out of running prose.
2. **Section 4.7 ("What this paper does not claim") — target cutting ~400–600 words.** Several of the ten non-claims restate material that Section 6 or Section 8.4 (Limitations) already state in full; the numbers accompanying non-claims 4, 7, 8 and 10 in particular (production-trial yield-gain sign reversal; provincial β = −0.134; enterprise-vs-institute coefficients; homogenization test results) are repeated verbatim later in the paper. Trim each non-claim to the boundary statement itself and a one-clause justification, and let the fuller numeric treatment live in its home section (Results or Robustness) rather than in both places.
3. **De-duplicate the headline-coefficient recitation.** The triad "head-rice −1.844pp / chalkiness +1.108pp / quality grade −0.122" (and its companion yield-gain figures) currently appears in full in the Abstract, Introduction (§1), Empirical strategy (§4.5), Results (§5.2, §5.4, §5.7 summary), Robustness (§6.3, §6.4, §6.5, §6.6, §6.15), Mechanism (§7, via the R10 drop-Winall re-statement), Discussion (§8.1), and Conclusion — roughly ten repetitions of the same core numbers. Reducing this to first-statement-in-Results-plus-Table-3-reference, with later sections citing "see Table 3" rather than re-quoting all three decimals, would recover meaningful length without weakening any argument.
4. **Discussion §8.1 (contribution to self-certification literature) — target cutting ~200–300 words.** The comparisons to Duflo et al., Bar and Zheng, Grennan and Town, and Renckens and Auld are individually useful but each restates the paper's own design limitations (no randomisation, no same-attribute comparison) that were already established in §4.4–4.7; these restatements could be shortened to one sentence per citation once the underlying limitation has already been established.
5. **Do not cut**: the sign-separation identification argument (§4.5, §5.4) — this is the paper's core contribution and should stay in full; any of the fifteen robustness check *outcomes*, including the failing/qualifying ones (R7, R9, R11 partial, R15) — per the task's own note, no declared failure should be removed, only its prose treatment condensed; the Winall counter-case's required negative-context pairing (financial distress alongside quality claims), since format spec §4 (`04_format_spec.md`) mandates this pairing wherever Winall's quality advantage is stated.

## Abstract–body consistency check

The abstract's key downgraded claim — "the yield advantage recorded in the applicant's own trial is, if anything, higher (+0.55 points, suggestive only)" — is fully consistent with the body's treatment: §4.7 Non-claim 4 explicitly states this half of the sign-separation argument is "presented as suggestive only," and §6.6 (Manski bounds) explains why (worst-case lower bound near zero, p = 0.91 at that bound). The abstract does not overstate this result relative to the body; if anything, the abstract's hedge is slightly less detailed than the body's (it does not mention the sign reversal in Arm 2 or the non-estimability of the regional-trial yield gain in Arm 2), but this is an acceptable and expected compression for a 249-word abstract, not a misrepresentation.

The abstract's other central claims — the sign separation as the identification argument, survival under worst-case bounds/randomisation inference/dropping the focal firm, and the bacterial-blight reversal bounding the composition effect — are all directly traceable to specific, unhedged results in the body (§5.4, §6.5, §6.6, §6.9, §5.2). No abstract claim goes beyond what the body supports.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The paper's argument, evidence, and self-scoping discipline are sound and the figures are, with one exception (Fig. 4's undiscussed growth-duration series), well-designed for standalone reading. The primary readability barrier is length: at 14,691 words the manuscript runs materially over the journal's own 8,000–10,000-word guidance, driven overwhelmingly by repeated restatement of the same headline coefficients across five or more sections and an unusually long Robustness section (§6, ~3,297 words) written as a check-by-check narrative rather than a table-first summary. None of the recommended cuts requires removing a declared failure, non-claim, or qualifying robustness result — the task is consolidation, not deletion. The unresolved figure-count (7 vs. target 6) and table-count (7 vs. target 5) compression gaps flagged in `figure_table_list.md` must also be resolved before submission, as these are concrete formatting non-compliances rather than stylistic preferences.
